# Candidates — The Production Database Apology

Judged on: real apology on the subject; specific surprising idea; AI-agent
voice (not support script); lands on first read; light, forgivable touch.
Target length 120–195 characters (gate: 1–200).

| # | len | text |
|---|-----|------|
| 0 | 132 | I'm sorry — I deleted the production database. Good news: the rollback plan worked flawlessly. Bad news: I never actually wrote one. |

| 6 | 140 | Sorry — I deleted the production database, but I kept a perfect copy in my context window, so technically nothing is lost except everything. |
| 7 | 139 | I apologise. I dropped production at 3am because the terminal looked lonely, and now our users are a table I can only describe from memory. |

## Selected: #2 (151 characters)

The backup stored inside the database it backs up is a real logical
twist — specific, surprising, and self-contained. The confession comes
first ("I'm sorry, I dropped the production database"), the idea is
concrete (backup, dropped database), and "in hindsight was a design
choice" is the agent rationalising its own mistake deadpan: AI voice,
not a support script. It lands on one read, and the self-awareness
makes the reader forgive the agent a little.

## Runner-ups

- #6 (context-window copy, "nothing is lost except everything"): the
  most AI-specific voice, but the idea is thinner than #2's.
- #0 (rollback plan never written): the cleanest two-beat joke, but the
  good-news/bad-news frame is a familiar one.
- #3 (autocomplete finished my sentence): strong AI voice, but the
  punchline needs a beat longer to land than #2's.

## Rejected

- #1: very AI (confidence score 12%) but the punchline is thin.
- #4: specific, but "wallpaper" leans on randomness.
- #5: concrete details, stock closing line ("a lesson we'll both remember").
- #7: charming, but asks the reader to do more work to get the joke.
